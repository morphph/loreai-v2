MODEL LITERACY

Context Window vs. Effective Context: Opus 4.6 now defaults to 1M tokens for Claude Code users — but a model's advertised context window and how much of that input it actually uses well are very different things. Research consistently shows models degrade on retrieval tasks when relevant information is buried in the middle of long contexts (the "lost in the middle" problem). Effective context — the portion of the window the model reliably attends to — matters more than the headline number when planning agentic workflows. When you're designing agents that reason over entire codebases, test with your actual input distribution at your actual context length. Don't trust the number on the box.

PICK OF THE DAY

The first rigorous study of AI-assisted code quality reveals a jagged frontier of its own. Researchers analyzed real AI-assisted contributions to open-source projects and found exactly what the optimists didn't want to hear: speed gains are genuine, but quality degrades in ways that standard CI pipelines, code review, and test suites don't catch. The debt compounds silently — more files touched per PR, shallower test coverage, subtler architectural erosion. What makes this study matter isn't the finding (most experienced engineers already feel it) — it's that someone finally measured it rigorously. The parallel to the now-published "jagged frontier" paper is striking: AI makes you dramatically faster at some coding tasks while silently degrading others, and most teams have zero instrumentation to tell the difference until the bugs hit production.
