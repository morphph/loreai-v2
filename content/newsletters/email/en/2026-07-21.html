🎓 MODEL LITERACY

Open Weights vs Open Source. Today's coverage of Kimi K3 and China's AI strategy keeps using "open-source" and "open-weights" interchangeably — but the distinction matters enormously. Open weights means you can download and run the model, fine-tune it, and deploy it in your own infrastructure. True open source means you also get the training code, dataset details, and full methodology — enough to reproduce the model from scratch. Most "open-source" AI models are actually open-weights: you can use them, but you can't recreate them. This determines whether competitors can merely deploy the model or actually build on top of the research. When someone says a Chinese lab "open-sourced" a frontier model, ask which one they mean — the answer changes everything about the competitive dynamics.

🎯 PICK OF THE DAY

Cursor's agent swarm economics reveals what most teams are optimizing wrong. The headline number everyone focuses on is per-token cost — but Cursor's internal framework shows that naive agent parallelization is a losing strategy for a completely different reason. When you run a swarm of agents in parallel, the compound effects of latency variance and per-call reliability dominate total system cost far more than the price of any individual token. A 95% reliable agent call sounds great until you chain ten of them and your end-to-end success rate drops to 60%. Multiply that by the latency tail — where your total wall-clock time is bottlenecked by the slowest agent in the swarm — and the economics flip. Most teams building agent architectures are optimizing the cheapest variable (token price) while ignoring the ones that actually determine whether the system works at scale (reliability compounding and latency distribution).
