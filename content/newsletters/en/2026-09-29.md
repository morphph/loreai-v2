# Sonnet 5.5 Speeds Up as OpenAI Shelves Astra and AMD Moves for World Labs

**2026-09-29**

**Claude Sonnet 5.5** makes a sharp speed and price pitch, while **OpenAI** withholds a model after a safety regression. Meanwhile, **AMD** is making an $8.2 billion bet on spatial AI.

Today: Sonnet 5.5’s speed and cost, Astra’s failed safety gate, and agent security and cost controls.

### Sonnet 5.5 Promises More Speed for Less Money

Routine AI work could get cheaper and quicker: **Claude Sonnet 5.5** runs more than 30% faster than Sonnet 5 and costs up to 30% less for most work, according to Anthropic. It’s the second model in the Claude 5.5 family and is available now. Those numbers deserve a test on your own workloads before you change your default model. That could make ongoing tasks easier to hand off; the test is whether those agents stay useful after the first assignment. ## 📝 TECHNIQUE

**Give coding agents repositories, not just prompts.** A useful instruction may live across reference repos, skills, and examples rather than in one prompt you can copy and paste. Point an agent at a few relevant projects before a task, then check whether its output improves. (3,520 likes | 118 RTs) [Read more →](https://x.com/trq212/status/2104608785696440510)

**One rules file for multiple coding agents** keeps instructions aligned. A shared agent setup uses a single `AGENTS.MD` and symlinks so Claude Code and Codex read the same rules. It’s a practical pattern if you keep fixing the same instruction in two places. (407 likes | 34 RTs) [Read more →](https://x.com/undefinedKi/status/2104656567505211685)

**Diagnose the workflow before rewriting the prompt.** A failing AI feature may need better retrieval or a different application design, not another round of wording changes. Audit where the failure occurs before you tune the instruction. (81 likes | 25 RTs) [Read more →](https://x.com/hrswatigupta/status/2104549724888146268)

## 💡 INSIGHT

### OpenAI Withholds Astra After Safety Tests Regress

A newer frontier model failed a consequential release gate: **OpenAI** says it will withhold GPT-6.1 Astra after it performed worse than GPT-6 Astra on safety tests. That makes the safety result a commercial decision, not a footnote beside capability gains. The harder question is whether the same standard holds when the next model offers an even stronger performance case. [Read more →](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/openai-will-ship-gpt-6-1-astra-due-safety-concerns)

### AMD Moves for World Labs in an $8.2 Billion Deal

**AMD** is pursuing an $8.2 billion acquisition of Fei-Fei Li’s **World Labs**, linking its hardware business to 3D simulation models. The proposed deal suggests AMD wants a stake in the workloads its chips may run, not just the chips themselves. Watch whether the acquisition closes and how AMD connects spatial models to its platform. [Read more →](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/amd-buy-fei-fei-lis-world-labs-8-2-billion)

**Cheaper intelligence could change agent design.** Rich project context and dynamic workflows consume tokens, but a post argues that Sonnet and Opus 5.5 change that cost calculation. If your team stripped context to save money, rerun the numbers before treating those limits as permanent. (1,236 likes | 26 RTs) [Read more →](https://x.com/trq212/status/2104660926373023830)

## 🎓 MODEL LITERACY

**Safety regression testing** asks whether a new model does worse than its predecessor on safety checks, even when it improves elsewhere. OpenAI’s reported GPT-6.1 Astra decision turns on that comparison with GPT-6 Astra. A release gate gives the test teeth: a regression can stop a launch. The hard part is keeping that rule when the model also promises capabilities customers want.
